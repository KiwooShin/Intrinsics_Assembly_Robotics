Learning Sub-Millimeter Connector Insertion — and proving where it hits a wall

A UR5e learns to insert SFP/SC fiber-optic plugs into NIC-card ports (~2 mm bore, sub-millimeter clearance) in a Gazebo simulation scored 0–100 per trial. The learned policy reaches the port reliably; the final seat does not. This is the study of why — a systematic, literature-grounded elimination that turns a negative result into a hard root cause.

The task, and why it is hard

Each trial spawns a task board at a randomized pose. The arm, holding a fiber plug, must reach the correct port and seat it. Scoring is tiered: tier 1 for reaching the workspace, tier 2 for proximity to the port mouth, tier 3 for a genuine insertion (a physics touch-latch that welds the plug). The bore is about 2 mm with sub-millimeter clearance — a classic high-precision peg-in-hole, but with two constraints that decide everything:

  • The last inch is occluded. As the plug approaches, the plug body itself blocks the cameras' view of the port.
  • Ground-truth port pose is training-only. Reading the port's /tf during evaluation is disqualifying "state leaking" — the deployed policy must localize from its own sensors.

The system that was built

The deployed stack is an Action-Chunking Transformer (ACT) visuomotor policy — three RGB views plus joint proprioception in, short action chunks out — layered with a force-aware guarded descent and a learned port-bearing head that estimates the residual port direction at a stall. A key early fix unlocked the whole approach:

Controller root cause. The stock MODE_VELOCITY integrates the reference open-loop, so the arm drifts and freezes. Switching the deployment to receding-horizon MODE_POSITION took the officials from 36 → 119 / 300. This is why the policy reaches proximity at all.

Ablation — the learned port-bearing head is essential to the guarded recovery

Officials, n=3 each, matched seeds, ensembling off. Removing the aux-bearing head (leaving only the scripted guarded descent) collapses two of three officials to near-zero — the scripted descent pushes in the wrong direction without a learned bearing.

The insertion investigation

Reaching the mouth is solved; closing the final ~13 mm laterally is not. Rather than tune endlessly, the work treats "can this rig seat a plug?" as a set of falsifiable hypotheses and eliminates them one by one. Five independent method families, zero seats on the hard poses:

The money figure — localization error is capped by camera resolution, not just occlusion

Held-out port-localization error vs. true distance-to-port. If occlusion alone were the problem, error would fall sharply once the port is fully visible (far frames). It does drop — but only to 22.7 mm, still 4–5× above the force-reactive capture radius that a compliant seater can close. The 128 px cameras simply cannot resolve a 2 mm port at any range.

The negative result, stated precisely. The seat is not blocked by a tuning gap or a training-data shortfall — it is blocked by a structural sensor limit: no pre-contact bearing to the 2–5 mm precision every successful method requires. That conclusion is only trustworthy because five independent approaches were built, run, and falsified — including a disassembly-reversal pipeline whose two real bugs (a physics touch-latch welding the plug during data collection, and a time-reversal ordering error) were found and fixed before the method was fairly rejected.

Engineering along the way

  1. A scoring-frame bug that made an entire task class unwinnable. SC configs targeted a port frame name that did not exist, so the scoring TF lookup failed and every SC trial collapsed to 1.0/100 for any policy, including a perfect one. Root-caused to the <target_module>/<port_name>_link TF construction and fixed to the real sc_port_base frame across 30 configs + the suite generator + a regression test. Oracle score on a fixed SC pose: 1.0 → 93.
  2. Quantified the simulator's noise floor. Same config + seed varies sd 3–18 points with proximity↔miss flips — so single-trial comparisons are meaningless. Every adoption decision uses n≥3 with IQM and bootstrap CIs.
  3. Hardened the evaluation harness. Process-group-scoped teardown, resumable batch evals with DONE markers, and detached-job orchestration — so a 48-hour autonomous run survives crashes and heartbeat gaps.

What this demonstrates

A working visuomotor manipulation policy that reliably solves the reachable part of a contact-rich task; a force-aware recovery whose learned component is validated by ablation; and — the core of the work — a disciplined, falsifiable investigation that converts "it doesn't seat" into a specific, literature-anchored statement about the sensing the platform is missing. Knowing exactly why something is impossible on a given rig, and proving it, is as much an engineering result as making it work.
