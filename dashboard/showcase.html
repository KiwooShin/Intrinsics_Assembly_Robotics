Sub-millimeter connector insertion: a structural wall, and the curriculum that got past it

A UR5e learns to insert SFP/SC fiber-optic plugs into NIC-card ports (~2 mm bore, sub-millimeter clearance) in a Gazebo simulation scored 0–100 per trial. Two results, both earned honestly: the blind full task is capped by a structural sensor limit — proven by falsifying five method families. Then, by reframing the question as a curriculum, the project produced its first learned-policy seats and a clean, literature-matching study of exactly how the seating skill generalizes — and where it breaks.

The task, and why it is hard

Each trial spawns a task board at a randomized pose. The arm, holding a fiber plug, must reach the correct port and seat it. Scoring is tiered: tier 1 for reaching the workspace, tier 2 for proximity to the port mouth, tier 3 for a genuine insertion (a physics touch-latch that welds the plug) — a seat scores ~88–94/100. The bore is about 2 mm with sub-millimeter clearance — a classic high-precision peg-in-hole, but with two constraints that decide everything:

  • The last inch is occluded. As the plug approaches, the plug body itself blocks the cameras' view of the port.
  • Ground-truth port pose is training-only. Reading the port's /tf during evaluation is disqualifying "state leaking" — a deployed policy must localize from its own sensors.

The deployed stack is a deterministic action-chunking visuomotor policy: three RGB views + joint proprioception + wrist force/torque in, a short chunk of end-effector twists out (K=4), trained by L1 regression over demonstrations. (It is an ACT-style chunker — it does not use a CVAE latent; that distinction turns out to be the crux of Act 2.) A key early fix unlocked everything: the stock MODE_VELOCITY controller integrates its reference open-loop and drifts, so the arm freezes mid-reach; switching deployment to receding-horizon MODE_POSITION is what lets the policy track a target at all.

Act 1 — the blind task

The seat is blocked by a sensor limit, not a tuning gap — proven by elimination

Reaching the mouth is solved; closing the final millimeters laterally, blind, is not. Rather than tune endlessly, the work treats "can this rig seat a plug from a free start?" as a set of falsifiable hypotheses and eliminates them one by one. The money figure — localization error is capped by camera resolution, not just occlusion

Held-out port-localization error vs. true distance-to-port. If occlusion alone were the problem, error would fall sharply once the port is fully visible (far frames). It does drop — but only to 22.7 mm, still 4–5× above the force-reactive capture radius a compliant seater can close. The 128 px cameras cannot resolve a 2 mm port at any range.
The negative result, stated precisely. The blind seat is not blocked by a tuning gap or a data shortfall — it is a structural sensor limit: no pre-contact bearing to the 2–5 mm precision every successful method requires. That conclusion is trustworthy because five independent approaches were built, run, and falsified — including a disassembly-reversal pipeline whose two real bugs (a physics touch-latch welding the plug during collection, and a time-reversal ordering error) were found and fixed before the method was fairly rejected.
Act 2 — the curriculum

Reframe the question: isolate the seating skill, then study how it generalizes

If the blind approach is sensor-capped, stop fighting it and ask a different, answerable question: given the plug already positioned above the port, can a policy learn the seat itself — from vision and force alone? A curriculum makes this concrete. During training only (legal here — this is a capability study, not a scored submission), the plug is privileged-staged ~20 mm above the port; the policy then learns to descend and seat from live 3×RGB + pose + wrist F/T, and the lateral offset is grown stage by stage.

This immediately produced the first learned-policy insertions in the project — every prior seat had come from the privileged scripted oracle.

Mapping the capture radius — and a dose-response experiment

With aligned insertion solid, the natural question is how far off-center can the policy start and still seat? Sweeping the staged lateral offset gives a clean answer for the base specialist (trained on pure-vertical oracle demos): a capture radius of [1, 2) mm — reliable at 1 mm, and beyond ~2 mm the policy drifts away rather than searching, exactly because its demonstrations contain no rim-contact recovery to imitate.

So the next experiment adds that missing content: late-correction demos (descend at the offset, correct laterally near the mouth, then seat) are folded into the corpus in increasing amounts — 0, then 9, then 18 episodes (checkpoints m3c → m3d → m3e). The result is the centerpiece below.

The dose-response — capture translates outward, it does not expand

Seats (of 3) at each staged offset as late-correction demos are added. As the frontier is pushed out, 2 mm success climbs 0 → 1/3 → 2/3 while 1 mm reliability erodes 3/3 → 2/3 → 1/3. The two curves cross: the operating point is being shifted outward (center ≈1.5 mm), not widened. Monotone and anti-correlated across three checkpoints (joint-pattern Monte-Carlo p≈0.007 under a noise null); seats score 92–94 vs misses 0–43, a >49-pt gap the 3–18-pt scoring noise cannot flip.
1 mm offset (reliable inner band, erodes) 2 mm offset (frontier, advances)
Root cause — this is textbook, and it names the fix. The deployed head is a deterministic L1 regressor (verified: opt/train_v3.py uses F.l1_loss; the model is a CNN encoder + MLP, no CVAE / latent / KL anywhere). An L1 head learns the conditional-median action — it structurally cannot hold "descend straight" and "translate then descend" as two modes at one near-mouth state. So adding outward-search demos does not widen the window; it makes the median try to serve two regimes. The aligned mode is retained (0 mm 3/3) and a 2 mm mode is gained (2/3), but at the 1 mm boundary between them the single median blends both into neither — a dead-zone (1/3). This is exactly the unimodal-BC mode-averaging that motivated ACT's CVAE (2304.13705) and Diffusion Policy (2303.04137). The literate remedies follow directly: a genuinely multimodal action head, or residual RL on a frozen base (IndustReal 2305.17110, ResiP 2407.16677, IBRL 2311.02198) so wide capture is an additive correction that cannot overwrite the aligned descent.

The engineering conclusion is a mixture of specialists: keep m3c as the reliable near-aligned seater (3/3 @ 94) and treat m3e as a 2 mm specialist (2/3 @ 92), routed by the early-descent contact signal — you discover the trade-off, then engineer around it rather than pretending one median can be in two places at once.

Engineering & rigor

What kept the results honest

  1. A scoring-frame bug that made an entire task class unwinnable. SC configs targeted a port-frame name that did not exist, so the scoring TF lookup failed and every SC trial collapsed to 1.0/100 for any policy, including a perfect one. Root-caused and fixed to the real sc_port_base frame across 30 configs + the suite generator + a regression test. Oracle score on a fixed SC pose: 1.0 → 93.
  2. A scoring-write race that silently razed valid seats. The engine writes scoring.yaml after the task-end log marker; tearing the sim down on the marker discarded late-written scores — biasing exactly the slow, drift-then-recover trials. Fixed by waiting on the file itself before teardown; a naïve grep 'score:' that had been reading tier-1's 1.0 and hiding tier-3 seats was fixed to parse total+tier_3.
  3. A memory leak that masqueraded as a failed experiment. A retrain "failed" and every eval crashed with a missing-checkpoint error. The real cause: 76 leaked aic_adapter + 106 leaked static_transform_publisher orphans (~0.5 GB each, one per past trial) ate ~90 GB of unified memory, OOM-killing training. Purged (→57 GB free), widened the teardown patterns, retrained cleanly. Lesson banked: never run a training in a foreground call (a 2-min timeout killed one) — always detached + logged.
  4. Quantified the simulator's noise floor. Same config + seed varies sd 3–18 points with proximity↔miss flips — so single-trial comparisons are meaningless. Every adoption decision uses n≥3, and the dose-response claim rests on the joint monotone pattern across three checkpoints, not any single cell.

What this demonstrates

A working visuomotor policy that reliably solves the reachable part of a contact-rich task; the project's first learned-policy seats, isolated through a deliberate curriculum; a force ablation that shows which sensor carries the contact phase; and two disciplined, falsifiable investigations that each convert a raw outcome into a specific, literature-anchored mechanism — a structural sensor limit for the blind task, and unimodal-BC mode-migration for the capture radius. Knowing exactly why something breaks on a given rig, proving it, and naming the fix is as much an engineering result as the seat itself.
