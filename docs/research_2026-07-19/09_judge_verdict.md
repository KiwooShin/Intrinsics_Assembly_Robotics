# Adversarial Feasibility Judgment — Three Plans to >90/trial

## 0. Two framing facts that reset the scoring baseline

**(a) The target is two different problems, and the plans conflate them.** The **official /300** prize (+160, 119→~279) is almost entirely *one seat on three already-reached poses* — cheap, ~2–4 days, high-probability for all three. The **stratified >90 average** requires coverage-hole closure (10/15 miss) + SC-collision elimination + *last-inch reliability everywhere* — and the only method in any brief with a demonstrated path to ≥90% reliability across poses is **residual RL** (Phase 3 in all three). So every plan's ">90 avg" claim is really an RL claim, and RL wall-clock is the true gate (see §2).

**(b) Competition intel is load-bearing and under-weighted by all three.** Qualification (this exact 3-trial /300) **closed May 15**; live Phase-1 is the diverged `phase_1` branch (multiple SC ports/rail — *harder* SC disambiguation, the plans' weakest area — plus PR #595's contiguous-force penalty). Today is **2026-07-19**; participant-stated Phase-1 submit is **~Jul 28** (~9 days). Every plan's full calendar (7–10 days) overruns Jul-28-with-buffer, and all three validate on the stale `main` snapshot. A feasibility judge must treat this as a scope cap: **only the officials-prize path (~4 days), ported to `phase_1`, is realistically shippable live.** The >90-everywhere plans are research goals, not submission plans, within this window.

## 2. Weakest-assumption attack, each plan

**Plan A — weakest link: "Phase 3 coverage lifts stratified to ~85–93 *without* residual RL."** Counter-evidence from the briefs: BC+DAgger+coverage has produced **0/45 insertions** across every checkpoint; the Data-Audit's controlled A/B (Phase-2 added 33 targeted demos → *best-ever val L1* → **still 0/45, worst officials**) is direct proof that more same-shaped data does not convert. MimicGen-replay and assembly-by-disassembly lift *reach* (miss→proximity) but the forensics ceiling for "everything reachable seats" is **~68**, and only IndustReal/AutoMate/HIL-SERL (all **RL**) reach 83–100%. A's own trajectory table quietly assumes the coverage phase does RL's job. **A reaches ~279 official reliably; its ~90 *stratified* number is the plan's soft spot.** (A is honest enough to kill-gate here, which is why it still scores highest.)

**Plan B — weakest link: "one 17-min retrain swaps the head and tests the thesis."** Two counter-hits. (i) *Engineering:* a flow-matching action head on a bespoke 0.75M CNN is not a config flip — it's a new sampler, loss, decoder-widening, and normalization, i.e. days of build+debug before the first *valid* 17-min retrain; the primitive (I read it as a scripted controller in C) is comparably cheap and far lower-variance. (ii) *Mechanism:* the Method brief is explicit that a multimodal head on a bimodal terminal **"risks drawing 'stop' as often as 'push,'"** and only helps *paired with* wrench/tail-curation — at which point the distribution is no longer bimodal and **the head is doing little the data fix isn't**. B's bundling of head+wrench+tail means a positive result can't distinguish "the head worked" from "the tail-drop worked," and the ACT scripted-data ablation predicts the latter. B leads with the highest-cost, least-attributable lever.

**Plan C — weakest link: "wrench is non-degenerate in Gazebo AND a bounded XY spiral seats without tripping −24."** Counter-evidence: (i) *Every trial in the entire record has `force_score = 0`* — the sim has *never* exercised a force penalty, so contact-force dynamics at seating are **completely unvalidated**; if Gazebo's contact model gives a flat/noisy Fz (plausible given the `#489 restore SC-plug↔port contact` / `#454 lower cable friction` churn), C's P1/P2/P3-reward all collapse in one shot. (ii) The **6 clean −23 collisions are all SC into `nic_card_mount` distractors**, and `phase_1` adds *multiple SC ports per rail* — a blind XY spiral in that clutter is exactly the geometry that produces −24, turning the primitive net-negative on precisely the configs C needs it for. C's single-point-of-failure is larger than A's, though its payoff (fast officials seat) is the surest of the three.

## 3. Best-of-breed composition

The plans are more complementary than competitive; the right build takes **A's discipline + C's seat mechanism + B's head as a gated contingency**, sequenced by cost-of-information:

- **Phase 0 (shared, ½ day) — superset of all three.** Take **B's harness hardening** (fix the 1070 s `completed=False` hangs, one-command n≥3 matched-seed IQM/CI/insertion-count) + **the shared wrench-fidelity probe** + **A's stall-state logger** + **C's Z-stiffness sweep + budget extension to full window**. This is mandatory infra and resolves the crux gating all three (§4).
- **Phase 1 (the productive fork, run both — they don't contend for GPU):**
  - **C-Phase-1 force-guarded primitive** = the officials +160 prize, scripted/train-free, eval-legal (RGB+wrench, no GT), mirrors the #1 (Datameister) and best-classical (MacCody) teams. This is the *submission spine.*
  - **A-Phase-0 relabel (drop seated tail) + weighted-L1 + wrench-in-state** = a near-free retrain that *also* tests the aliasing thesis and improves the *learned* approach the primitive hands off from. GPU-serialized but the primitive dev is GPU-free, so they interleave.
- **Phase 2 (shared):** **SC-oracle repair FIRST** (floor≈−0.007 + pose-conditioned waypoint, re-validate zero-contact — teacher bug, identical across A/B/C) → then **A/B's last-inch auto-DAgger** *and* **MimicGen-style privileged-pose replay** into the |yaw|∈[0.5,1.5] + SFP-port_0 + rail0 holes. This buys *approach reach* everywhere so the primitive can fire on stratified poses.
- **Phase 3 (ceiling, post-submission):** **off-policy residual RL** on the frozen approach+primitive base, reward = engine insertion event. Shared across all three; the *only* path to >90 stratified — but see §2 wall-clock.

**Where they genuinely conflict:**
1. **Head-swap ordering (A/C vs B).** Evidence (ACT scripted ablation; B's own non-attributable bundling) favors A's rule: **escalate to a flow/diffusion head only if BOTH the data-relabel and the primitive fail to seat.** B's opener becomes the composite's *contingency #1*, not the lead.
2. **Learned-seat vs scripted-seat (A/B vs C).** If you build the primitive (C), the DAgger/head investment in *learned* seating is partly redundant *for the officials*. Resolution: **primitive owns the seat (officials, now); BC+DAgger+coverage owns approach-reach (stratified); RL owns reliability (ceiling).** Don't pay twice for the last inch in the BC policy while the primitive already seats.
3. **RL wall-clock (unspoken, all three).** Off-policy residual RL needs ~10²–10³ rollouts; at **6.5 min/trial wall** that is **~15 h (100 rollouts) to ~4.5 days (1000)** *of pure rollout, serialized against training that drops RTF to 0.05×*. HIL-SERL's "14→64% in 15 min" is *real-time* robot data and **does not transfer** to this throughput. So >90 stratified is not reachable inside the Jul-28 window under any plan; treat it as Phase-2 (Aug 4+) work.

## 4. Highest-information-per-hour FIRST experiment

**Run a ~4-hour scripted "guarded-descent-from-the-stall-state" probe on the three officials, n≥3 each, logging wrench.** Concretely: drive v2_wide's learned approach to its observed 0.05–0.08 m stall on official_1/2/3, then hand off to a compliant descent that commands a MODE_POSITION target **placed below the current TCP, into the port axis** at low Z-stiffness (the mechanical fix for the re-anchor attractor I verified in `DeployACT.py`), while recording `/fts_broadcaster/wrench` and the engine's insertion/−24 events.

This single experiment collapses the most uncertainty across *all three plans at once*:
- **Resolves the shared wrench-fidelity crux** (Phase-0 kill in A, B, and C) — the one signal on which A's wrench-state, B's wrench-obs, and C's entire thesis + P3 reward-shaping all depend, and which the record has *never* exercised (`force_score=0` everywhere).
- **Directly tests whether the officials prize is a control problem** — i.e. whether the arm can physically seat when *explicitly commanded into the port from the handoff pose*. If **yes** → ~279/300 is ~2 days away via the scripted primitive, and the entire "multimodal head vs DAgger" debate is **deprioritized for the submission**. If **no** (won't seat even when driven in) → the barrier is approach-alignment/perception at the handoff pose, which redirects *everyone* away from head/last-inch work toward the encoder/coverage.
- **Measures the −24 exposure** of a descent in the port neighborhood before any spiral logic is built.

It is higher-information than B's "swap the head and retrain" (days of build, non-attributable result) and than A's full Phase-0 retrain (tests aliasing but not the *seat ceiling*), because it answers the one question every plan's officials trajectory silently assumes — *can this arm seat at all from where the learned policy parks it* — for the cost of a scripted controller and three-config n≥3 eval, no training run. It is essentially **C-Phase-0 + the decisive slice of C-Phase-1**, and it should be the composite plan's day-1 move regardless of which thesis ultimately wins.
